Our Story

It started with a cart on the sidewalk.

Back in 1916 this corner was a grocery store. Somewhere along the way the owners rolled a cart onto the sidewalk and started selling hamburgers to people walking by on Racine Street. The burgers outlasted the groceries, and the cart became a permanent little stand.

The name over the window has changed a few times — Becker's, then Armstrong's, then Peterson's — but each new owner bought the recipe along with the building and changed almost nothing. When longtime customers took it over and put the Wedl name up, they kept the same rule: same meat, same onions, same skillet, same spot.

When the stand was damaged in 1999, the original griddle turned up about half a block down the street. It was cleaned up, restored, and put right back to work. It's still the surface your burger is cooked on today.

The Stand

Sixty-four square feet, one cast iron skillet.

There is no dining room. There are a few stools, a window, and a griddle that has been seasoned by a hundred years of burgers.

The Griddle
  • A century-old cast iron surface that has never really been replaced.
  • The day starts with lard on the griddle, then burgers and onions go down.
  • Grease from the griddle is spooned over the onions as they cook.
The Building
  • About 64 square feet — smaller than most walk-in coolers.
  • Right on U.S. 18 / Racine Street, a block east of Business 26.
  • Order at the window; grab a stool if one is open.
Good to Know
  • Cooked to order — weekends can mean a line down the sidewalk.
  • Hours are seasonal. A quick phone call saves a wasted trip.
  • Parking is on the street, right out front.
